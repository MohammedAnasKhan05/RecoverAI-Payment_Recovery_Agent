# UPI PAYMENT RETRY & RECOVERY POLICY

## 1. Scope

This policy applies to failed, pending, or unsuccessful UPI payment transactions, particularly merchant payments where the payment may still be recoverable.

The policy is intended to guide automated recovery decisions while preventing excessive retries, duplicate payment attempts, and inappropriate automated actions.

---

## 2. Failure Classification

UPI failures should first be classified according to the reason for failure.

### Retryable / Transient Failures

The following failures may be considered for a controlled retry:

* Network timeout
* Gateway timeout
* Temporary bank/PSP unavailability
* Transient technical failure
* Temporary communication failure

A retry should only be considered when the transaction has not already been successfully completed or settled.

### Non-Retryable Failures

The following conditions should generally NOT trigger an automatic retry:

* Incorrect UPI PIN
* Account blocked or restricted
* Insufficient funds
* Explicit user rejection
* Fraud or suspicious transaction indicators
* Permanent bank/account-related failures
* Retry limit already reached

NPCI notes that repeated incorrect UPI-PIN attempts may result in a temporary block, depending on the bank.

---

## 3. Retry Decision

A UPI transaction may be automatically retried only when:

* The failure is classified as transient.
* The transaction has not already succeeded.
* Fraud risk is below the configured threshold.
* The retry limit has not been reached.
* The transaction is within the merchant's permitted recovery window.
* No conflicting payment confirmation exists.

Example:

FAILURE_REASON = timeout
PAYMENT_METHOD = UPI
FRAUD_RISK < 70%
ATTEMPTS < MAX_RETRIES

→ RETRY

---

## 4. Retry Limits

RecoverAI must enforce a configurable retry limit.

For the MVP:

* Maximum automatic retries: 2
* After the retry limit is reached → STOP automatic recovery.
* Further action requires escalation or manual review.

The limit is an application-level safety rule for RecoverAI and should not be presented as a universal NPCI rule.

Specific UPI flows can have different operational retry rules. For example, NPCI's UPI Reserve Pay guidance permits acquiring entities to retry certain timeout/debit scenarios up to 3 times within 24 hours, while stating that other declines should not be retried.

---

## 5. Transaction Status Verification

Before executing another recovery attempt, RecoverAI should verify the latest transaction status.

If the transaction is already successful:

→ STOP all recovery actions.

If the transaction is pending:

→ Do not immediately create another payment attempt.

→ Wait for status confirmation or follow the applicable reconciliation process.

NPCI explains that UPI transaction status can remain pending when there is a delay at the beneficiary-bank end.

---

## 6. Customer Account Debited but Merchant Not Confirmed

If the customer's account has been debited but the merchant has not received confirmation, RecoverAI must NOT blindly retry the payment.

The system should:

1. Check the transaction status.
2. Attempt reconciliation/status verification.
3. Avoid creating a duplicate debit.
4. Escalate if the final status cannot be determined.

RBI's failed-transaction framework provides specific auto-reversal timelines for UPI. For a merchant payment where the account is debited but confirmation is not received at the merchant location, the framework specifies auto-reversal within T+5 days, with compensation for delays beyond the prescribed timeline.

---

## 7. Customer Account Debited but Beneficiary Not Credited

For UPI fund transfers where the customer's account is debited but the beneficiary account is not credited, the transaction should be handled through status verification and reversal/reconciliation rather than blindly retrying.

RBI's framework specifies auto-reversal by the beneficiary bank within T+1 day when the beneficiary cannot be credited.

---

## 8. High-Risk Transactions

Automatic retry must be blocked when:

* fraud_probability > 70%
* transaction is flagged as suspicious
* transaction has unusual risk characteristics
* customer or merchant risk exceeds the configured threshold

Action:

→ ESCALATE

The transaction should be sent for manual review rather than automatically retried.

---

## 9. High-Value Transactions

For RecoverAI's automated recovery workflow:

* Transactions above ₹50,000 require additional review.
* Automatic recovery should not bypass the high-value guardrail.
* The transaction should be escalated when the configured threshold is exceeded.

Action:

→ ESCALATE

This ₹50,000 threshold is a RecoverAI business-rule/guardrail for the MVP, NOT an NPCI UPI transaction limit.

---

## 10. Recovery Probability

RecoverAI should estimate the probability that another recovery action will successfully recover the revenue.

If:

recovery_probability >= 70%

→ Strong candidate for recovery.

If:

30% <= recovery_probability < 70%

→ Controlled recovery / additional evaluation.

If:

recovery_probability < 30%

→ STOP automatic recovery.

The exact thresholds are RecoverAI's internal decision rules and should be configurable.

---

## 11. Action Recommendations

### UPI + Timeout

→ RETRY

Only when risk and retry guardrails pass.

### UPI + Temporary Technical Failure

→ RETRY

Only when the latest transaction status confirms that another attempt is safe.

### UPI + Incorrect PIN

→ STOP

Do not automatically retry.

### UPI + Insufficient Funds

→ SEND_REMINDER or STOP

Allow the customer to resolve the issue before another attempt.

### UPI + Fraud/Suspicious Risk

→ ESCALATE

No automatic retry.

### UPI + Retry Limit Reached

→ STOP

Manual intervention may be required.

### UPI + Debited but Merchant Confirmation Missing

→ VERIFY_STATUS

Do not blindly retry.

---

## 12. Recovery Action Hierarchy

RecoverAI should follow this priority:

1. Check transaction status.
2. Check fraud/risk score.
3. Check retry count.
4. Check transaction amount.
5. Check recovery probability.
6. Retrieve applicable UPI policy.
7. Generate AI recommendation.
8. Apply deterministic guardrails.
9. Execute the approved recovery action.
10. Record the result in the audit log.

---

## 13. Stopping Rules

RecoverAI must immediately stop automated recovery when:

* Payment becomes successful.
* Fraud risk exceeds the threshold.
* Retry limit is reached.
* Transaction status becomes uncertain.
* Transaction has already been debited successfully.
* Recovery probability falls below the minimum threshold.
* A high-value transaction requires manual approval.
* The payment failure is classified as non-retryable.

---

## 14. Audit Requirements

Every recovery attempt must record:

* Transaction ID
* Timestamp
* Payment method
* Failure reason
* Attempt number
* Fraud probability
* Recovery probability
* Retrieved policy
* AI recommendation
* Guardrail decision
* Final action
* Recovery result
* Recovered amount

Example:

TRANSACTION: TX001
FAILURE: UPI timeout
FRAUD RISK: 2%
RECOVERY PROBABILITY: 87%
POLICY: UPI PAYMENT RETRY POLICY
AI ACTION: RETRY
GUARDRAIL: PASSED
RESULT: SUCCESS
RECOVERED: ₹4,999

---

## 15. RecoverAI Default Decision

For the MVP:

IF payment_method = UPI
AND failure_reason = timeout
AND fraud_probability < 70%
AND attempt_number < 2
AND recovery_probability >= 30%
AND transaction_status != SUCCESS

→ RETRY

Otherwise, apply the appropriate escalation, stop, verification, or alternative recovery rule.

---

## 16. Compliance Note

RecoverAI is an automated decision-support and recovery system.

Its internal retry limits and risk thresholds are application-level controls and do not replace applicable NPCI, RBI, bank, PSP, or payment-provider rules.

Where transaction status, reversal, refund, dispute, or reconciliation is involved, the system should follow the applicable payment-system process rather than attempting an additional payment blindly.

NPCI provides official UPI circulars and operating guidance, while RBI specifies customer-protection and failed-transaction turnaround requirements.
